Spark Plug Replacement
Spark plug inspection, gapping, and installation
14 Lessons~65 min total · Easy
1Overview

This guide covers the spark plug replacement procedure — why plugs matter for combustion efficiency — the same procedure taught in the Spark Plug learning module, practice simulation, and assessment simulation.

What you will learn:

  • How spark plugs work and the signs that they need replacement.
  • The tools required including a spark plug socket and torque wrench.
  • A 10-step procedure to safely replace spark plugs.
2Tools & Materials
New spark plugs (matching OEM specification)
Spark plug socket (5/8" or 16 mm) with extension bar
Torque wrench
Spark plug gap gauge
Anti-seize compound and dielectric grease
Safety Reminder: Always work on a fully cooled engine. Hot spark plug threads can cross-thread or cause burns.
3Procedure — 10 Steps
1Let the Engine Cool30 min waitEasy
Allow sufficient cooling time before touching any engine components.
  1. Turn off the engine and open the hood.
  2. Wait at least 30 minutes for the engine to fully cool before proceeding.
Installing spark plugs into a hot engine can cause thread damage or aluminum head warping — never rush this step.
2Locate the Spark Plugs3 minEasy
Find each spark plug on the engine — the number matches the cylinder count.
  1. Follow the ignition wires or coil-on-plug connectors leading from the ignition system to the engine head.
  2. Each wire or coil corresponds to one spark plug — work one at a time.
3Remove the Ignition Wire or Coil3 minEasy
Disconnect the ignition wire or coil-on-plug from the first spark plug.
  1. For ignition wire: grip the rubber boot — not the wire — and twist while pulling straight out.
  2. For coil-on-plug: unclip the electrical connector, then remove the retaining bolt and lift the coil straight up.
Replace plugs one at a time to avoid mixing up the firing order — label wires if necessary.
4Remove the Spark Plug3 minEasy
Use the spark plug socket to unscrew and extract the old plug.
  1. Insert the spark plug socket with extension bar into the plug well.
  2. Turn counter-clockwise to loosen, then remove the plug by hand for the final turns.
  3. Keep the plug upright to avoid debris falling into the cylinder.
5Inspect the Old Spark Plug3 minEasy
Read the condition of the old plug to identify any engine issues.
  1. Light tan or grey deposit — normal wear, replacement is due.
  2. Black sooty deposit — rich mixture or oil fouling, investigate further.
  3. Melted or blistered electrode — engine overheating or incorrect heat range.
6Check the Gap3 minEasy
Verify the new spark plug gap matches the vehicle's specification before installation.
  1. Slide the correct feeler gauge blade between the center and ground electrode.
  2. The gauge should pass with slight resistance — check the spec in your owner's manual (typically 0.8–1.1 mm).
  3. Bend the ground electrode gently with a gap tool to adjust if needed.
An incorrect gap causes misfires, poor fuel economy, and hard starting — always verify before installing.
7Apply Anti-Seize Compound2 minEasy
Coat the plug threads lightly with anti-seize to ease future removal.
  1. Apply a very thin coat of anti-seize compound to the first two or three threads only.
  2. Do NOT apply anti-seize to iridium or platinum plugs — check the plug manufacturer's recommendation first.
8Install the New Spark Plug3 minEasy
Thread the new plug into the cylinder head carefully by hand first.
  1. Place the new plug into the socket and lower it carefully into the plug well.
  2. Thread in by hand (clockwise) until finger-tight — this prevents cross-threading.
Never use the ratchet to start threading — cross-threading an aluminum head is a costly mistake.
9Torque to Specification3 minEasy
Use a torque wrench to tighten the plug to the manufacturer's specified torque.
  1. Set the torque wrench to the specified value (typically 15–25 Nm — confirm in vehicle manual).
  2. Tighten until the torque wrench clicks — do not over-tighten.
Over-tightening can crack the porcelain insulator or strip the threads. Always use a torque wrench.
10Reattach Wire or Coil & Test5 minEasy
Reconnect the ignition wire or coil-on-plug and start the engine to verify operation.
  1. Apply dielectric grease inside the boot, then press the wire boot or coil firmly onto the plug until it clicks.
  2. Repeat Steps 3–10 for each remaining spark plug.
  3. Start the engine — listen for smooth idling with no misfires or rough running.

5Key Takeaways
Always work on a cool engine — never install plugs into a hot head.
Replace plugs one at a time to maintain the correct firing order.
Verify the gap before installing and torque to specification with a torque wrench.
Inspect the old plug condition — it can reveal underlying engine problems.
