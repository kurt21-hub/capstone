Battery Maintenance
Battery inspection, service, and safe replacement
15 Lessons~35 min total · Easy
1Overview

This guide covers battery maintenance — why it is critical for vehicle reliability — the same procedure taught in the Battery learning module, practice simulation, and assessment simulation.

What you will learn:

  • The role of the battery and the signs of failure.
  • The correct order for disconnecting and reconnecting battery cables.
  • An 11-step procedure to safely service and replace a vehicle battery.
2Tools & Materials
Replacement battery (matching CCA rating and size)
Wrench set (typically 10 mm)
Wire brush or battery terminal cleaner
Baking soda and water solution (for corrosion)
Safety glasses and acid-resistant gloves
Safety Reminder: Batteries contain sulfuric acid and produce hydrogen gas. Always wear eye protection and gloves, and work in a ventilated area away from sparks.
3Procedure — 11 Steps
1Safety Precautions3 minEasy
Review critical safety rules before working on the battery.
  1. Turn off the ignition and remove the key.
  2. Put on safety glasses and acid-resistant gloves before touching the battery.
  3. Ensure no open flames or smoking near the battery — hydrogen gas can ignite.
Do not place metal tools across the battery terminals — this can cause a dangerous short circuit and arc flash.
2Locate the Battery2 minEasy
Find the battery in the engine bay or trunk depending on the vehicle model.
  1. Open the hood and look for a rectangular plastic case with two cable terminals (red and black).
  2. On some vehicles the battery is in the trunk or under a rear seat — check the owner's manual.
3Inspect the Battery3 minEasy
Assess the battery condition before deciding whether to clean or replace it.
  1. Check the terminals for white or blue corrosion buildup.
  2. Look for a swollen or cracked case — this indicates the battery must be replaced.
  3. Check the manufacture date label; batteries older than 3–5 years are nearing end of life.
4Disconnect the Negative Cable2 minEasy
Always remove the negative (−) cable first to prevent short circuits.
  1. Identify the black cable connected to the terminal marked "−" or "NEG".
  2. Use a 10 mm wrench to loosen the clamp nut.
  3. Wiggle the clamp free and move the cable away from the battery.
Negative first, positive last — the universal rule for battery disconnection to prevent sparks.
5Disconnect the Positive Cable2 minEasy
Remove the positive (+) cable after the negative is safely set aside.
  1. Identify the red cable connected to the terminal marked "+" or "POS".
  2. Loosen the clamp nut with a wrench and remove the cable from the terminal.
  3. Keep the positive cable away from any metal surfaces.
6Remove the Battery3 minEasy
Unbolt the battery hold-down bracket and carefully lift the battery out.
  1. Remove the hold-down bracket bolt or clamp at the base of the battery.
  2. Lift the battery straight up — keep it level to avoid spilling acid.
  3. Set it on a flat, stable surface away from the vehicle.
Car batteries are heavy (15–25 kg). Lift with your legs, not your back. Do not tilt — acid may leak from vent caps.
7Clean the Battery Tray & Terminals5 minEasy
Remove corrosion from the battery tray and cable terminals before installing the new battery.
  1. Mix one tablespoon of baking soda with one cup of water.
  2. Apply the solution to corroded areas on the tray and clamps, then scrub with a wire brush.
  3. Rinse with clean water and dry thoroughly with a rag.
8Install the New Battery3 minEasy
Place the new battery into the tray with the correct terminal orientation.
  1. Lower the new battery into the tray, matching the positive terminal to the positive cable side.
  2. Reinstall the hold-down bracket and tighten securely — the battery must not move.
A loose battery can shift during driving and cause terminal shorts or damage to the engine bay.
9Connect the Positive Cable2 minEasy
Reconnect the positive (+) cable first when installing a battery.
  1. Slide the red cable clamp onto the positive (+) terminal.
  2. Tighten the clamp nut firmly with a wrench — the cable should not rotate on the post.
10Connect the Negative Cable2 minEasy
Reconnect the negative (−) cable last to complete the circuit safely.
  1. Slide the black cable clamp onto the negative (−) terminal.
  2. Tighten the clamp nut securely.
  3. Apply a thin coat of terminal grease or petroleum jelly to both terminals to prevent future corrosion.
Positive first, negative last — the universal rule for reconnection to prevent sparks.
11Test and Verify5 minEasy
Start the engine and verify the battery and electrical system are working correctly.
  1. Start the engine and verify it cranks smoothly without hesitation.
  2. Check that all dashboard warning lights extinguish normally after startup.
  3. Test electrical components — headlights, wipers, and radio — to confirm full power.

5Key Takeaways
Always remove negative first, reconnect negative last — this prevents dangerous sparks.
Clean corrosion with baking soda solution and a wire brush before fitting the new battery.
Secure the hold-down bracket firmly — a loose battery is a safety hazard.
Apply terminal grease to prevent future corrosion and ensure good electrical contact.
