Brake Pad Replacement
Brake pad inspection, removal, and installation
20 Lessons~75 min total · Easy–Medium
1Overview

This guide covers the complete brake pad replacement process from start to finish — the same procedure taught in the Brake Pad Replacement learning module, practice simulation, and assessment simulation.

What you will learn:

  • Why regular brake pad replacements are essential for vehicle safety.
  • The tools and materials required for a safe brake pad change.
  • A 16-step procedure from preparation to completion.
2Tools & Materials
Hydraulic jack and jack stands
Lug wrench or impact drill
New brake pads
C-clamp or caliper piston compressor tool
Socket set
Bungee cord or zip tie
Safety Reminder: Always wear gloves and safety glasses. Brake dust can be harmful.
3Procedure — 16 Steps
1Prepare the Vehicle5 minEasy
Park on a flat surface, engage parking brake, block the wheels.
  1. Park on a flat, level surface and engage parking brake.
  2. Place wheel chocks behind the rear tires.
Never work on a slope, and ensure the vehicle is perfectly stationary.
2Loosen the Lug Nuts5 minEasy
Break the lug nuts loose before raising the vehicle.
  1. Use a lug wrench to perform a quarter-turn counterclockwise on each lug nut.
  2. Do NOT remove the nuts completely while the vehicle is on the ground.
3Raise the Vehicle5 minMedium
Use a jack to lift the car and secure it with jack stands.
  1. Position the hydraulic jack on the manufacturer-recommended pinch weld.
  2. Lift until the tire clears the ground completely.
  3. Place a jack stand under a secure frame rail and lower the car onto it.
4Remove the Wheel2 minEasy
Fully unscrew the lug nuts and take the wheel off.
  1. Completely undo the loosened lug nuts.
  2. Pull the wheel straight out.
  3. Place the wheel flat under the car frame for extra protection.
5Inspect Rotor & Caliper3 minEasy
Examine the brake rotor and caliper for scoring, wear, or fluid leaks.
  1. Run your finger over the rotor — check for deep grooves or pitting.
  2. Inspect the caliper boot for brake fluid leaks.
6Remove Caliper Bolts7 minMedium
Unbolt the brake caliper from its mounting bracket.
  1. Locate the two slider pins/bolts on the back of the caliper.
  2. Use your socket set to loosen and remove the bolts.
7Secure the Caliper3 minEasy
Hang the caliper out of the way safely.
  1. Slide the caliper off the rotor.
  2. Use a bungee cord or a zip tie to hang the caliper securely from the suspension spring.
8Remove the Old Pads3 minEasy
Pop the old brake pads out of the mounting bracket.
  1. Note how the metal retaining clips are placed.
  2. Slide old pads straight away from the rotor.
  3. Keep one old pad to use for compressing the piston next.
9Inspect Mounting Bracket2 minEasy
Ensure the bracket hardware is in good condition.
  1. Remove old retaining clips.
  2. Use a wire brush to clean the bracket.
10Compress the Caliper Piston5 minMedium
Push the caliper's hydraulic piston back into its housing.
  1. Place your old brake pad directly against the piston face.
  2. Position a C-clamp so it squeezes the back of the caliper and the old brake pad.
  3. Tighten the clamp until the piston is flush with the caliper housing.
11Apply Brake Grease3 minEasy
Lubricate parts to prevent brake squeal.
  1. Apply a small amount of brake caliper grease to the slider pins.
  2. Apply grease to the backing plate of the new pads, avoiding the friction material.
12Install the New Brake Pads5 minEasy
Snap the new pads into the bracket.
  1. Snap on the new retaining clips into the bracket.
  2. Slide the new pads squarely into the mounting bracket against the rotor.
13Reinstall the Caliper5 minEasy
Swing the caliper back down over the new, thicker brake pads.
  1. Remove the bungee cord / zip tie from the caliper.
  2. Slide the caliper over the new pads. Ensure you do not twist the brake hose.
  3. Re-insert the slider bolts and tighten snugly with your socket wrench.
14Reinstall the Wheel3 minEasy
Put the wheel back on the vehicle.
  1. Lift the wheel back onto the lug studs.
  2. Hand-thread all lug nuts in a star-pattern to hold it securely.
15Lower the Vehicle5 minMedium
Set the car back to the ground securely.
  1. Jack the car up slightly to remove the jack stand.
  2. Slowly release jack pressure.
  3. Tighten the lug nuts heavily in a star-pattern.
16Pump the Brakes2 minEasy
Rebuild brake pressure before shifting out of Park.
  1. Sit in the driver's seat.
  2. Pump the brake pedal firmly 4–5 times until it feels very stiff.
DO NOT drive without pumping the brakes first.

5Key Takeaways
Always secure the vehicle and never rely on a hydraulic jack alone.
Never stretch or dangle the brake lines.
Compressing the piston is essential to fit new, thicker pads.
Always pump the brake pedal several times to restore brake pressure.
