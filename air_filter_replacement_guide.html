Air Filter Replacement
Air filter inspection and replacement — inspection to reinstallation
12 Lessons~25 min total · Easy
1Overview

This guide covers the complete air filter replacement process from inspection to reinstallation — the same procedure taught in the Air Filter learning module, practice simulation, and assessment simulation.

What you will learn:

  • Why a clean air filter is critical for engine performance and fuel efficiency.
  • The tools and materials needed for an air filter replacement.
  • An 8-step procedure to safely replace the engine air filter.
2Tools & Materials
New replacement air filter (check manual for size)
Flathead screwdriver or socket set (housing clips/bolts)
Clean cloth or shop rag
Compressed air or vacuum (optional)
Safety Reminder: Always work on a cool engine. Never replace an air filter immediately after driving.
3Procedure — 8 Steps
1Locate the Air Filter Housing2 minEasy
Find the air filter housing in the engine bay before starting any work.
  1. Open the vehicle hood and secure it with the prop rod.
  2. Locate the air filter housing — typically a large black plastic box connected to a large rubber intake hose.
Ensure the engine is off and fully cooled before reaching into the engine bay.
2Inspect the Old Filter2 minEasy
Before removing, assess the condition of the existing air filter.
  1. Unclip one side of the housing lid to peek at the filter.
  2. Hold the filter up to the light — if you cannot see light through the folds, it must be replaced.
  3. Check for oil contamination, insects, or physical damage.
3Disconnect the Air Intake Hose3 minEasy
Loosen the clamp securing the rubber intake hose to the housing.
  1. Use a flathead screwdriver or socket to loosen the hose clamp screw.
  2. Gently twist and pull the rubber hose away from the housing port.
Do not force or kink the intake hose — it can crack and cause an air leak affecting engine performance.
4Open the Filter Housing2 minEasy
Unclip or unscrew the air filter housing lid to access the filter inside.
  1. Release all spring clips or unscrew the bolts holding the housing lid in place.
  2. Lift the lid away carefully and set it aside without straining any attached sensor wires.
5Remove the Old Air Filter2 minEasy
Lift the old filter out of the housing without shaking debris into the intake.
  1. Carefully lift the old filter straight up out of the housing.
  2. Avoid tilting or shaking it — loose debris can fall into the intake manifold.
  3. Place the old filter in a bag immediately for disposal.
Cover the open intake port with a clean cloth while the housing is open to prevent debris ingestion.
6Clean the Housing3 minEasy
Remove any dirt, dust, or debris from inside the air filter housing before fitting the new filter.
  1. Use a clean dry cloth to wipe out the interior of the housing.
  2. If available, use compressed air or a vacuum to remove fine dust particles.
  3. Inspect the housing for cracks or damage that may cause air leaks.
7Install the New Air Filter2 minEasy
Place the new filter correctly into the housing with the rubber seal facing down.
  1. Verify the new filter matches the old one in size and shape.
  2. Lower the new filter into the housing, ensuring the rubber gasket seats evenly all the way around.
  3. Press down gently to confirm a snug, even fit.
8Close and Secure the Housing3 minEasy
Reattach the housing lid and reconnect the intake hose to complete the installation.
  1. Place the housing lid back on and fasten all clips or bolts firmly.
  2. Reattach the rubber intake hose to the housing port and tighten the hose clamp.
  3. Start the engine and listen for any unusual whistling or air-sucking sounds indicating a leak.
Never run the engine with the air filter housing open — unfiltered air can damage the engine internals.
4Knowledge Check
Q1How can you tell if an air filter needs replacement without removing it?
Q2Why should you cover the intake port while the housing is open?
Q3What does a whistling sound after reassembly indicate?
Q4Why must the rubber gasket of the new filter seat evenly?
5Key Takeaways
A clogged air filter reduces engine power and fuel efficiency — replace every 15,000–30,000 km.
Always cover the open intake port when the housing is removed.
Ensure the filter gasket seats evenly to prevent unfiltered air bypass.
Test the engine after reassembly and listen for air leaks.
